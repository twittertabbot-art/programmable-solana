Custom hooks

Get your hook reviewed

A hook from outside the platform opens a pool here only after Programmable reviews its code and approves the program.

What the mark means

An approved hook carries this mark in Custom hooks and on its page.

Reviewed by Programmable

What it says

The hook passed the ten review rules below.

The program on chain is frozen. Its bytes are the build of the commit that was reviewed.

The mark carries the date of the review and links to its report.

What it does not say

A review is not an audit and not a promise.

It checks the ten rules and nothing else. It does not say that a coin on the hook is worth buying.

Read the hook's report before you trade or launch on it.

When a hook breaks the rules later

Programmable takes it off the approved list. No new coin can then launch on it.

Coins already on it keep it, because a pool's hook can never change. Their pages show a warning.

The review rules

A hook must meet every one of them to be approved.

  1. Public source, pinned

    The code is in a public repository at one commit. Its license lets anyone read it and build it.

  2. Frozen, and the reviewed code

    The program's upgrade authority is removed. Its bytes on chain are the verified build of the reviewed commit.

  3. Sells always go through

    If the hook runs on sells, nothing in it can fail a sell because of who sells, when or how much. Only the errors any trade can meet are allowed, such as wrong accounts, slippage or a paused platform.

  4. Fees bounded in code

    Every fee the hook can charge has a ceiling fixed when the pool is created, and the coin's page shows it. A fee someone can change after launch moves only between zero and that ceiling, and the report names who can change it. The hook may change the pool fee of a trade only on a schedule in its code that goes down over time, as a sniper fee does. No key can send the fees to another wallet after launch.

  5. No power that hurts holders

    The report names every setting someone can change after launch. None of them can block trades, lift a fee above its ceiling or take funds the hook holds for others, such as a buyback pot or rewards.

  6. Written once

    No instruction rewrites the hook's manifest or its list of extra accounts after setup. A rewritten list could stop every pool on the hook at once.

  7. Asks only for what it uses

    The permission flags in its manifest match what the code does.

  8. Calls only what it names

    The report names every program the hook calls.

  9. Checks its caller and its settings

    It accepts a call only from the pool program's caller address for its own id. A pool's settings belong to the wallet that created that pool.

  10. Fits

    A trade with the hook stays well inside the compute budget. The hook does not grow its accounts without bound.

A hook that fails any rule is not approved. The platform's own hooks have reports of their own, written the same way.

What it costs

Deploying your program

Solana keeps this as rent for the program's bytes. You pay it to the network from your own wallet when you deploy.

A frozen program never gives its rent back.

The review fee

25 USD

Reading the SOL price

You pay it to Programmable, in SOL, when you send the request.

It pays for the review whatever the outcome. A changed program is a new request with a new fee.

From your code to an approved hook

Six steps. The kit in hook-template of the package builds, tests, deploys and freezes a hook.

  1. 01Build and test

    Build the hook with the kit and test it on your own machine.

  2. 02Publish the code

    Put the code in a public repository and note the commit.

  3. 03Send a request

    Fill in the form below and pay the review fee from your wallet. Then open a ticket in Programmable's Discord with the request id the page gives you.

  4. 04The review

    Programmable reviews that commit and answers in your Discord ticket. Your request's page shows the status.

  5. 05Deploy and freeze

    Once the code passes, deploy exactly that commit with the kit's release steps. Write the verified build record, then freeze the program. Give its program id on your request's page.

  6. 06Approved

    Programmable checks on chain that the program is frozen and is the build of that commit. It then goes on the approved list, and anyone can launch a coin on it.
