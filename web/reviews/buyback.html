Buyback and burn

A report on one of the platform's own hooks, written from its code.

A share of every buy and every sell collects in a treasury that only the hook controls. Once it holds the minimum, anyone can run a buyback. A run buys the coin from its own pool and burns all it bought.

Its code is programs/hook_buyback/src/lib.rs in the package.

    What it does

    The launcher sets a buy share, a sell share and a minimum when the coin launches. The pool takes the share of each trade in the quote token, SOL on this site, and pays it to the hook's treasury for this coin.

    Once the treasury holds the minimum, anyone can call execute_buyback. A call buys the coin through the pool with a slice of the treasury, then burns every coin it bought. The buy runs no hook and pays no hook fee.

    Who controls the program

    This is one of the platform's own hooks. Its upgrade authority is a key of Programmable that the platform whitelists, so the pool takes it without approval.

    Programmable's key can still upgrade it. An upgrade could change anything this report says, the use of the treasury included. Programmable answers for it as it answers for the pool program itself.

    What it can take, and where it goes

    A buy pays the buy share. A sell pays the sell share. The program refuses a share above 10%, and the pool cuts it to the platform's cap, now 10%. Both shares are fixed at launch.

    The shares go to the treasury. It is a token account of an address that belongs to the hook and to this one pool. Only the hook's code can sign for it. The pool checks at creation that its hook treasury is this account, and nobody can change that later.

    The treasury is spent only by execute_buyback, and only on a buy of the coin through the pool. Every coin bought is burned in the same call. Each call is held to these limits:

    • It runs only while the treasury holds the minimum, at most 1 SOL, set at launch.
    • It buys only against a record of the pool at least 25 slots old. The first call only records the pool.
    • When the price is above the record by more than twice the cap, it records the pool again and buys nothing.
    • It spends at most a slice of the smaller of the pool's quote reserve now and the recorded one. The slice follows the coin's fees and stays between 0.01% and 0.5%.

    Can it refuse a trade

    No. It refuses no buy and no sell because of who trades, when or how much. Its call before a trade can fail only on a sum too large for any real pool.

    A trade fails only for reasons any trade meets, such as wrong accounts or slippage. It never runs when liquidity is added or removed.

    What can change after launch, and who changes it

    No setting changes after launch. The shares, the minimum and the treasury have no owner and no instruction that changes them.

    InstructionWhoWhat it does
    execute_buybackanyoneBuys the coin with a slice of the treasury and burns it, within the limits above. The caller sets the least the buy must return. A call that comes too soon fails and changes nothing.
    init_pool_configthe launcher, onceCreates the settings and the treasury before the pool exists.
    init_manifestthe upgrade authority, onceWrites the manifest and the list of extra accounts.
    on_pool_create, before_swapthe pool onlyThe calls above. A trade may lower the hook's record of the price, never its record of the pool's depth.

    The treasury holds funds for the coin's holders. Nobody can take them out. The only way out of it is a buy and a burn.

    Its manifest and its account list

    init_manifest writes both once. It needs the program's upgrade authority, and an account that exists cannot be created again. No instruction rewrites either of them. The list names one account, the coin's buyback settings, which the pool passes writable so that a trade can lower the record of the price.

    Programs it calls

    The pool program, for the buy of a buyback. The token program, to burn the coins. The associated token program and the system program, to create its two token accounts at setup. Itself, to record each buyback as an event.
