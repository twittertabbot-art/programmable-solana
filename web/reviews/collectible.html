Collectible per buy

A report on one of the platform's own hooks, written from its code.

Every buy earns the buyer pieces, NFTs in the coin's collection. A wallet keeps one piece per set amount of coins it holds. The hook takes no fee and never refuses a trade.

Its code is programs/hook_collectible/src/lib.rs in the package.

    What it does

    After a buy, the hook records how many pieces the buy earned. A buy earns one piece per set amount of coins received, up to a maximum per buy, the collection's maximum supply and 200 pieces per wallet. The hash of a block two slots later decides what each piece looks like. Pieces are minted as Metaplex Core NFTs, by the wallet or by a keeper paid from the wallet's own deposit.

    The pieces are pegged to the coin. When a wallet's coins no longer back its pieces, the extra pieces leave it. Waiting pieces are dropped first, then minted pieces are burned from the end of the wallet's list.

    A coin is one of two kinds, fixed at launch. On a plain coin the hook sees buys only, and anyone can send settle to remove the pieces a wallet's coins no longer back. On a pegged coin, a Token-2022 coin, the same program runs inside every transfer of the coin and moves or burns pieces there.

    Who controls the program

    This is one of the platform's own hooks. Its upgrade authority is a key of Programmable that the platform whitelists, so the pool takes it without approval. Only a hook of the platform's own can be the transfer hook of a coin.

    Programmable's key can still upgrade it. An upgrade could change anything this report says. Programmable answers for it as it answers for the pool program itself.

    Its manifest flags are AFTER_SWAP, ON_POOL_CREATE, CALL_ON_BUY.

    What it can take, and where it goes

    Nothing from a trade. It has no flag for a fee and gives the pool no answer.

    Outside the trade a buyer pays the rent of a ticket, once, and the NFTs of the pieces. A buy on this site adds a deposit to the buyer's ticket for those NFTs. A keeper that mints a wallet's pieces takes back what the mint cost plus 10,000 lamports from that deposit, and mints into that wallet only. Only the wallet can withdraw the rest.

    When a piece is burned, its NFT's rent goes to whoever sent the burn, by settle or reap.

    Can it refuse a trade

    No. A buy that can earn no piece earns none and still goes through, for example when the buyer has no ticket, the wallet is full or the supply is used up. Inside a transfer of a pegged coin it returns success for every real transfer, sells and sends included. When a piece has nowhere to go, it stays where it is instead of failing the transfer.

    Three of its own instructions, stage, reorder and send_pieces, are refused for a wallet whose coins no longer back its pieces. They are not trades.

    The coins per piece, the maximum supply and the maximum per buy are fixed at launch. No instruction changes them. The coin's page shows whether the collection is locked and who can update it.

    Its manifest and its account lists

    init_manifest writes the manifest and the pool's list of extra accounts once. It needs the program's upgrade authority, and an account that exists cannot be created again. The list names the coin's settings, the buyer's ticket and the recent block hashes. A pegged coin also has the list a token transfer reads, and a burn queue, both created once by init_peg. No instruction rewrites any of them.

    Programs it calls

    Metaplex Core, to create the collection and the pieces, to burn and move them, and to update the collection. The system program, for accounts and rent. The token program, in send_pieces, for a transfer the wallet signs.
